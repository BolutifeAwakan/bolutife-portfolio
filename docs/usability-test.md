# Usability sessions: comparing the two directions

Use this alongside the live A/B test. With a personal site's traffic, the
sessions will usually tell you more, sooner, than the numbers.

## Who and how many

- 6 to 8 people, a mix of the site's real visitors: founders or executives who
  hire design leadership, event organisers, designers who want to grow into
  leadership, and peers.
- 30 minutes each, on their own phone if possible (most visitors arrive on a phone).
- Half see the Brand Book first, half the Personal Editorial first. Alternate:
  participant 1 = A then B, participant 2 = B then A, and so on.

## Script

Say: "This isn't a test of you. I'm testing two designs for my website.
Please think out loud. There are no wrong answers."

For each direction, ask for a first impression after 10 seconds on the home
page, then give the six tasks. Don't help unless they're stuck for 60 seconds.

| # | Task (read it as written) | Done when they reach |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Find a brand identity project she has done and open it. | Any case study page |
| 2 | What is her experience as a design leader? Tell me in a sentence. | About, practice or experience section |
| 3 | What is Design Lever, and what is her role in it? | Ventures, Design Lever entry |
| 4 | Find something she has written, or her book. | An article, the writing list, or the book |
| 5 | What does she enjoy outside work? | On the side |
| 6 | You want to book her to speak at your event. How would you get in touch? | Contact page with Speaking chosen, or the email link |

## What to record for each task

| Participant | Direction | Order (1st or 2nd) | Task | Completed (yes / with help / no) | Seconds | Where they went wrong | What they said |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## After both directions

Ask each question for A and for B, on a 1 to 5 scale, then "why?":

1. First impression: how much did you want to keep looking?
2. Credibility: how much would you trust her with an important brand or leadership role?
3. Personality: how well do you feel you know who she is?
4. Memorability: tomorrow, which one would you remember?
5. Navigation: how easy was it to find things?
6. Readability: how comfortable was the text to read?
7. Ease of use overall.
8. Which would you choose, and why?

## Reading the results

Keep two columns separate:

- **Observed performance:** task completion, time, and where people got lost. This is evidence.
- **Preference:** the ratings and quotes. Useful, but people often prefer the one they saw second, which is why the order is alternated.

A direction that people prefer but can't navigate needs fixing before it can win.
A direction that performs well but people find forgettable may still be the
right choice for credibility. Write down which of those matters more to you
before you look at the results.
